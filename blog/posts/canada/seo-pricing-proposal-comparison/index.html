SEO Pricing in Canada: How to Compare Proposals and Scope

Comparing SEO pricing in Canada is more useful when you compare the actual scope, implementation and evidence than when you compare a monthly number alone. An audit, a local visibility project and a website rebuild are different purchases, even if each proposal uses the word “SEO”.

Use this guide to understand what a quotation includes, identify hidden implementation costs and agree a project you can review. Explore the Canada SEO service approach for context.

Define the outcome before requesting quotations

Describe the business problem in a short brief. You may need to resolve indexing after a redesign, improve a valuable service page, connect a Business Profile with the website or repair a broken booking journey. Tell providers which service and market matter most and what data you can currently access.

Include the platform, content responsibilities and access limits. A quotation that assumes a developer and an in-house writer is not directly comparable with one that includes implementation and copy editing. Explain who can approve changes and how quickly the business can review them.

Separate the main types of work

Work typeQuestions the quote should answerUseful output
Standalone auditWhich URLs, systems and problems are reviewed?Findings, priorities and verification steps
Implementation phaseWho makes changes and what access is required?Released changes and a change log
Local SEOWhat actual services and areas are included?Accurate profiles and useful local pages
Content workWho supplies expertise, writes and approves?Approved page improvements and supporting resources
Website developmentWhat is migrated, integrated and maintained?A tested website and clear handover

A provider may combine several types, but the deliverables should remain visible. “Full SEO” is not a substitute for stating what will be done.

Look for the implementation gap

A report can identify a broken template without including the work to fix it. Before choosing the lowest quote, ask which recommendations the provider will implement and which require a separate developer, designer or content owner. Include that additional effort in your comparison.

For technical work, compare the audit scope with the WordPress repair checklist. A useful proposal should state whether the deliverable is a diagnosis, an implemented repair or both.

Check geography, language and currency assumptions

A Canadian project may involve one city, several genuine locations or a broader market. Explain actual coverage and whether English, French or another language is required by the audience. Language review and additional locations can affect work volume, but their inclusion should be explicit.

Confirm the quoted currency, payment schedule, included revisions and responsibility for third-party subscriptions. Ask whether the proposal includes relevant taxes rather than inferring that a dollar symbol means CAD or that every provider uses the same commercial terms. Obtain tax-specific advice from an appropriate professional when needed.

If a provider proposes many city pages, ask what distinct information will support them. The Ontario location-page guide explains why a fixed page quota is not a strategy by itself.

Evaluate how results will be explained

Ask for a sample reporting structure, not another client’s confidential data. The report should connect completed work with relevant search and enquiry observations, identify missing measurement and explain what can be concluded. A chart without dates, page context or task history is difficult to use.

Distinguish deliverables the provider controls from outcomes that depend on search systems and the market. Providers can commit to agreed work and communication. They cannot legitimately control a permanent organic position or guarantee that an AI assistant selects your brand.

Questions for a GEO or AEO proposal

  • Which customer questions and platforms are being assessed?
  • What website or business information will be improved?
  • How will factual claims and expert answers be reviewed?
  • What distinguishes a brand mention from a citation or enquiry?
  • Which follow-up checks are included?

Our AEO guide for Canadian B2B service businesses explains the content side of that brief. Compare it with the actual GEO service and AEO service deliverables rather than paying for terminology alone.

Use a small comparison worksheet

For each quote, record the problem, affected pages, deliverables, implementation owner, exclusions, access, currency, payment stages and verification. Leave unknown items blank and ask the provider to clarify them. A short list of specific questions is more useful than forcing different services into a single price ranking.

Choose a first phase that resolves the highest-priority uncertainty. That may be an audit, a selected set of implemented changes or a page strategy. An ongoing arrangement should explain what useful work follows and how you will review it.

Can you quote before seeing the website?

A provider can explain typical scope options, but a binding implementation scope needs enough information about the website, access and objectives. Ask which assumptions might change the quotation.
