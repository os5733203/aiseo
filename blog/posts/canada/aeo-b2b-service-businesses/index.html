AEO for Canadian B2B Services: Turn Expertise into Useful Answers

AEO for a Canadian B2B service business means organising expert information around the questions buyers need answered before contacting a supplier. It is most useful when it makes a complex service easier to evaluate, with evidence and a clear next step.

That is different from producing hundreds of short answers in the hope that an assistant repeats the company name. This guide explains a practical answer-led content project for consultancies, specialist service firms and other businesses selling expertise. See our AEO service for the delivery scope.

Map the buying decision, not just the topic

A broad topic such as “business consulting” is not a content brief. A buyer may need to understand suitability, inputs, responsibilities, deliverables, implementation and how alternatives compare. Ask the team which questions appear in discovery calls and which misunderstandings delay a useful conversation.

Group the questions by decision. A suitability question often belongs on the service page. A detailed method explanation may justify a supporting guide. A question about who performs the work belongs close to expert information. Each resource needs a purpose within the journey.

Use a question-to-page worksheet

Buyer questionBest starting locationEvidence to gather
Is the service right for this problem?Service pageActual scope and suitability criteria
What will we receive?Service or process sectionApproved deliverable examples
How is the work carried out?Method guideExpert explanation and review steps
Who is responsible?Expert and about informationAccurate identity and role
How do we start?Relevant enquiry routeThe information needed for a useful reply

The content strategy service can turn that worksheet into a page plan. It should also identify existing content worth improving so the project does not create several competing explanations.

Make answers concise without removing necessary detail

Start a section with a direct explanation, then add the conditions that a buyer needs. A short answer can be misleading when the service depends on the client’s systems, available information or responsibilities. Use examples to clarify those dependencies, and label hypothetical examples as hypothetical.

For instance, a fictional consultancy explaining an implementation workshop could state who attends, what information is required and what the client receives afterwards. This helps the buyer understand whether the workshop meets their needs and what preparation is required.

Collect material from the expert doing the work

Ask a named subject expert to explain the process, common failures and limits of the service. Retain the source material and review the final wording with that person. Identify the author accurately and keep their role consistent with the about and service pages.

Use genuine project examples only when permission and context are available. Explain the scope and period behind any stated result. If there is no publishable outcome, a transparent process explanation can still help a buyer; inventing a statistic does not improve credibility.

Connect AEO with technical access and GEO

An answer cannot support discovery if the page is inaccessible or difficult to find. Link it from the relevant service and supporting content, make the important text available in HTML and check the intended canonical. Use structured data to describe the actual page rather than to insert claims that visitors cannot see.

Google’s guidance states that no special AI schema or new machine-readable AI file is required for its AI search features. AEO is therefore a content and website discipline, not a guaranteed placement mechanism. The separate GEO review can assess how a defined set of generative answers represents the business.

Respect the actual Canadian audience

Confirm which market and language the business serves. English content is not automatically sufficient for a buyer expecting French information, but a bilingual website also needs real translation and review. Do not imply a local Canadian office merely because the service can be delivered remotely.

Our Canada market page describes remotely delivered SEO and website support. Businesses with a local service footprint can also use the Ontario page-planning guide to distinguish language needs from geographic coverage.

Measure usefulness and business contribution separately

Review whether priority questions have complete, accurate answers and whether customers can reach the relevant service. Then examine available search behaviour and qualified enquiries. If you run prompt checks, record the platform, question, date and observed cited pages. Keep those observations separate from completed business enquiries.

Do not use one favourable response as proof of stable AI visibility. For buying decisions, the Canadian SEO proposal guide can help compare research, expert review, writing, implementation and follow-up as distinct deliverables.

Should every question become a new page?

No. Many related questions belong together on a substantive service or method page. Create a separate resource only when it has enough distinct purpose and information to help the reader.
